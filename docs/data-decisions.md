# Data decisions

A dated log of decisions about training data for the pilot events. The evidence for
each entry is in `backend/scripts/pilot_verification/` (`rules_final.py`,
`events_final.py`, `reports/final_check.md`, `reports/candidates_check.md`).

## 2026-10-10 — pilot event rules (owner: Tin, data)

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monitor scope.** Training labels come from AirNow sites inside each event's bbox. The AQS sites within 50 km listed in PR #21's check are a reference only. | Training rows need hourly labels from sites inside the event's box; a peak at an AQS site 50 km away does not describe the box. |
| 2 | **40 km rule.** The nearest in-box AirNow site must be ≤ 40 km from the event's own FIRMS detections. | Beyond 40 km the box has no monitor close enough to see this fire's smoke. |
| 3 | **Widening rule.** If the nearest AirNow site within 40 km of the fire is outside the computed bbox, the bbox widens symmetrically along the needed axis or axes, just enough to include it plus 0.05°. The site and its distance are recorded as `bbox_adjust`. There are no hand-set bboxes. | This replaces the hand widening PE-001 needed in PR #26 with a rule that applies to every event. |
| 4 | **Tier rule.** The tier comes from the max peak daily mean over in-box sites (LST days with ≥ 18 h), against the EPA 2024 AQI PM2.5 breakpoints (`TIER_RANGES`). That site is the tier site. | The tier should reflect the worst smoke any in-box monitor measured, not the site with the most hours or a reputation. |
| 5 | **Elevation rule.** For non-Low tiers, the tier site's peak must be ≥ 2× its baseline and ≥ 10 µg/m³ above it. The baseline is the median of the 7 pre-start LST days with ≥ 18 h. With fewer than 3 such days, it is the 20th percentile of the site's in-window daily means. | A high tier has to come from this fire raising PM2.5, not from an already-smoky background. |
| 6 | **75% hours rule.** The best in-box site must report ≥ 75% of the window's hours. | Below that, too many hours have no label. |
| 7 | **20% other-fire rule.** Less than 20% of in-box FRP may come from other fires. | Above that, labels in the box can't be attributed to this fire.<br>PE-009 (18.3%) and PE-020 (19.2%) are near the limit; accepted because FIRMS `_SP` is final reprocessed data. |
| 8 | **PE-008 Carr window is 2018-07-31 → 2018-08-30** (was from 2018-07-23). | Weaverville was dark on the top-quartile days 07-26, 07-28, 07-29 and 07-30. The later start raises its hours from 74.0% to 93.1%. |
| 9 | **Relabels:** PE-002 Dixie High → High-Medium; PE-004 Park High-Medium → High; PE-007 Glass High → High-Medium; PE-013 Zogg Medium-Low → High; PE-019 LNU Low → Medium-Low. | These are the tiers the max in-box AirNow peaks give under rule 4. PE-013's regional-smoke exception in PR #21 doesn't survive a computed tier. |
| 10 | **Replacements:** PE-016 Rim (2013) → Chimney Fire (2016-08-13 → 08-26, San Luis Obispo). PE-017 Caldor early phase → Mosquito Fire first three days (2022-09-06 → 09-08, Placer/El Dorado). | Rim and Caldor have no AirNow site inside their tight box (nearest: San Andreas, 49.7 and 40.8 km). Chimney and Mosquito were the picks of `screen_candidates.py`: they pass every rule with tier Low and clash with no other event's window within 200 km. |
| 11 | **NOAA-21 and MODIS are excluded from fire features.** | The only live FIRMS path, `ingestion.firms_poc` (`backend/ingestion/tasks.py:96-99`), requests `VIIRS_SNPP_NRT` only, so training must use the same sensor. |
| 12 | **PurpleAir is deferred to v1.1.** v1 uses regulatory labels only. | PurpleAir point balance is unknown and an event listing costs ~5k points; v1 ships on regulatory labels and PurpleAir Barkjohn labels come in v1.1. |
| 13 | **Fire features use VIIRS SNPP only:** `_SP` archive in training, NRT in live inference. Ingestion keeps storing every `_SP` product tagged by satellite; the DATA-04 loader filters to SNPP. | Train/serve parity; SNPP is the only VIIRS sensor covering every event (SP from 2012); adding NOAA-20 later needs no re-ingest. |

## 2026-10-12 — ingestion windows, AQS codes and forecast-point scope (owner: Tin, data)

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 14 | **AirNow pulls the tight bbox over [start − 24 h, end + 24 h]** (`AIRNOW_LEAD_HOURS`, `AIRNOW_TAIL_HOURS`), with the existing time-offset padding. | The first hours of an event need PM2.5 lags back to `pm25_lag_24h`, and the last hours need labels up to the 24 h horizon (`max(ALLOWED_HORIZONS)`). |
| 15 | **NCEI pulls bbox + 50 km over [start − 24 h, end]** (`NCEI_MARGIN_KM`, `NCEI_LEAD_HOURS`). | Live inference uses the nearest station wherever it is, so training must see stations just outside the box. The weather join needs reports from before the first issue time. |
| 16 | **AirNow AQS codes are normalized to 9 digits** (`normalize_aqs_code`) before the monitor id and the time-offset lookup. | AirNow sends some sites with an `840` country prefix (Paradise, Auburn). Without normalization, a site could split into two monitors, and Red Bluff's offset would not apply to a prefixed code. |
| 17 | **DATA-04 forecast points are AirNow monitors located in California:** AQS state code 06, or a tribal (TT) site listed in `CA_TRIBAL_SITES` after its location is checked (today: Morongo School TT-582-1016, Riverside County). Out-of-state in-box sites stay in the DB but are not forecast points. Implemented by `in_california_scope` in `backend/app/core/scope.py`. | The project scope is California, and live inference serves California points. Tribal AQS codes carry no state, so each tribal site is checked by location before it is listed. |
| 18 | **Forecast points:** per event, the AirNow monitors with valid regulatory pm25 inside the tight bbox during [start, end] and in California scope (#17), placed at the monitor's coordinates; `forecast_point_id` = `monitors.external_id`. | A point at a monitor always has a label at distance 0 when that monitor reports, and the same id identifies the point across events. |
| 19 | **Issue times:** every UTC hour in [start 00:00, end 23:00], each with every horizon in `ALLOWED_HORIZONS` (1, 3, 6, 12, 24 h). | Hourly issue times match live inference, and the AirNow tail (#14) supplies labels up to end + 24 h. |
| 20 | **Inputs per event:** fires = `fire_detections` with `satellite = 'VIIRS_SNPP'` in `firms_bbox` × [`firms_start_date`, end]; PM2.5 history and labels = regulatory AirNow in the tight bbox × [`airnow_start_date`, `airnow_end_date`]; weather = `weather_observations` in `ncei_bbox` × [`ncei_start_date`, end], with station lat/lon from the geometry. Built with `build_feature_dataset(lookback_hours=72)`. | Each source's frame covers exactly the box and window its features need (#13–#15), and SNPP only keeps training the same as live inference (#13). |
| 21 | **Dedupe and groups:** one row per (`forecast_point_id`, `issue_time`, `horizon_hours`). `event_ids` lists every event that produced the row, and `pilot_event_id` is the first. `group_id` joins events whose [start, end] windows overlap (connected components). | Overlapping events (e.g. PE-001 and PE-013 share Red Bluff) would otherwise put the same labelled hour in two folds. |
| 22 | **Cross-validation is leave-one-group-out on `group_id`.** | Events in one group share hours and smoke, so a per-event split would leak between train and test. |

## 2026-10-12 — baseline model v1 (owner: Tin, data)

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 23 | **Model features** (`backend/ml/feature_columns.py`, `FEATURES`): fire (distance, bearing sin/cos, FRP and count within 200 km), weather (wind alignment, speed, direction sin/cos, temperature, RH, pressure, 1 h precipitation), PM2.5 lags (1/3/6/12/24 h), calendar (day of year, month, smoke season, local solar hour) and `horizon_hours`. Labels, label provenance, identifiers, coordinates, event and group columns, `event_tier` and `precip_prob_pct` are excluded; a test fails if any of them becomes a feature. | Every feature is known at issue time and available to live inference. Ids and coordinates would let the model memorise sites; `event_tier` is derived from the labels. |
| 24 | **Baselines:** persistence = `pm25_lag_1h` → `lag_3h` → `lag_6h`; climatology = median label per (horizon, local solar hour) from the training folds only. | They are the bars any model must beat. Persistence is the hard one at short horizons (`docs/ml.md` §5). |
| 25 | **XGBoost, one model per horizon,** trained on log1p(PM2.5); predictions are expm1 and clipped at 0. Grid: max_depth 4/6/8, with eta 0.05, up to 800 trees with early stopping on an inner group split, subsample 0.8, colsample 0.8, min_child_weight 5, seed 42. The setting is chosen by pooled out-of-fold MAE. | Smoke PM2.5 is heavy-tailed; log1p keeps a few extreme hours from dominating the squared loss. Per-horizon models let each learn its own lag/fire balance. |
| 26 | **CV is leave-one-group-out on `group_id`** (13 folds), scored on raw µg/m³: MAE, RMSE, R², skill vs persistence, EPA 2024 AQI category accuracy, and precision/recall/F1 (plus AUROC/AUPRC) for ≥ 35.5. Reported pooled, macro over groups, and per horizon, tier and group, on rows with persistence and on all rows. | Whole held-out groups simulate unseen fires (#22). The outer folds also choose the setting, so the scores are nested-ish, not a clean nested estimate. |
| 27 | **Final models are refitted on all rows** with the chosen setting, one per horizon, and saved with the feature list, params, metrics, dataset sha256 and git commit (`backend/data/models/baseline_v1/`, not in git). They are loaded with `ml.inference.predict.predict(feature_frame, horizon)`. | A model is only usable if it can be traced to its data and code. The inference path checks the feature columns, so the serving and training contracts stay the same. |

## 2026-10-12 — baseline model v2, AQS codes in the DB, docs alignment (owner: Tin, data)

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 28 | **v2 predicts a residual on persistence:** per horizon, `log1p(y) − log1p(persistence)` on rows with persistence; prediction `clip(expm1(log1p(persistence) + r̂), 0)`. Rows without persistence use the v1 direct log1p model. | Trees cannot extrapolate beyond the training range. Anchoring on the last reading fixed v1's collapse on the held-out 2020 group (skill −0.255 → +0.028). |
| 29 | **Derived trend features live in one shared function,** `ml.features.add_derived` (`persistence`, `trend_1_3`, `trend_1_6`, `ratio_1_24`), called by both training and `predict_v2`. A test checks the two produce identical model input. | One function means training and serving cannot compute them differently. |
| 30 | **The exceedance cut-off is tuned on the inner validation split** of each outer fold (20–50 µg/m³, step 0.5, max F1 for ≥ 35.5). The served threshold is the median of the 13 fold cut-offs. | A threshold chosen on the test fold would inflate F1. v2 under-predicts the tail, so a fixed 35.5 under-alarms (recall 0.56 → 0.72 when tuned). |
| 31 | **Release rule, fixed before any v2 result:** per horizon, ship the model only if its MAE skill against persistence is ≥ 0 both pooled and macro over groups; otherwise serve persistence (`ml/evaluation/release.py`, `release.json`). | A model that loses to "nothing changes" on the whole data or on the typical fire should not serve. Fixing the rule first keeps the choice from being tuned to the result. |
| 32 | **`monitors.aqs_code` is the source of truth for AirNow AQS codes** (migration 0003; the connector writes it; `scripts/backfill_monitor_aqs_code.py` fills older rows). `dataset_build/airnow_sites.csv` is only a fallback, used with a warning. | The DB keyed monitors by a hash of the code, so the code itself was not queryable. A committed CSV would drift from the DB as new sites appear. |
| 33 | **Docs follow the code:** artifacts live in `backend/data/models/<name>/` (`docs/ml.md`). `docs/schema.md` describes observed NCEI weather at issue time (not NWS forecasts), the dataset path `backend/data/datasets/`, and the added columns. | Readers should not have to reconcile the docs with the code to know what a model saw. |

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 34 | **Live rows are marked by `ingestion_run_id`** on `observations`, `fire_detections` and `weather_observations` (migration 0006, no FK). Live jobs set it; DATA-03 batch rows keep NULL. Retention deletes only marked rows, and live upserts never modify an unmarked one. | The same tables hold the pilot-event rows `smokesense_v1` is built from, and their timestamps say nothing about where a row came from. A FK with `ON DELETE SET NULL` would turn live rows into "historical" ones when `ingestion_runs` is pruned. |
| 35 | **The live window is 72 h for all three tables** (`LIVE_RETENTION_HOURS`), fires included (schema.md said 7 days), enforced by a daily row DELETE (on by default; `LIVE_RETENTION_DELETE=0` or `--dry-run` for a dry run, changed in review 2026-10-26), not by dropping partitions. | Fire features look back 72 h, PM2.5 lags 24 h and weather 3 h. A daily partition can hold historical rows too, so dropping one is unsafe. |
| 36 | **Live weather comes from the NCEI training stations on NWS**: the 114 distinct batch `station_id`s, 109 of which exist on NWS under the same ICAO id (checked 2026-10-23). KCQT, KEKA, KGXA, SFD and STO have no NWS station and are not replaced (`backend/docs/live-ingestion.md`). KLHM and KMYV exist on NWS but had no observations on 2026-10-23. | The serving contract needs the same stations and nearest-station rule as training. A substitute picked by hand would be a station the model never saw under that id; the nearest-station rule already falls back to the next station. |
| 37 | **NWS: only METAR reports are stored** (an observation with `rawMessage` or the `precipitationLastHour` group); 5-minute ASOS readings are skipped. Every METAR time is stored as reported, specials included. | NCEI hourly data (training) has routine reports, not 5-minute readings, which carry no sea-level pressure or precipitation and would almost always be the "latest report within 3 h". Known gap: NCEI dropped specials (FM-16); NWS does not label them, so live keeps them. |
| 38 | **AirNow live cleaning is the training cleaning** (`normalize_airnow_row`): `RawConcentration`, 9-character AQS codes, per-site offsets, −999 and < −5 dropped, [−5, 0) clamped to 0; permanent monitors in `in_california_scope` only. Each run re-fetches 3 h before the last success so revisions update the stored row. `valid_at` is the start of the averaging hour, UTC. | Lags must be computed from the same values the model was trained on. AirNow revises preliminary hours after first publishing them. |
| 39 | ~~**PurpleAir live is a map layer**: one `/v1/sensors` call per 30 min, six fields, the sensor's current reading stored under the hour it falls in, Barkjohn-corrected with A/B QA. Not a model input (#12).~~ **Superseded by #41: live PurpleAir dropped.** | Points are billed per field per sensor; per-sensor history calls for thousands of sensors would cost far more. A current corrected reading is what the map shows. |
| 40 | **`/meta` freshness comes from `ingestion_runs`**: `last_success_at` is the newest `success` or `partial` run, `stale` means that run finished more than `stale_after_minutes` ago (defaults AirNow 150, NWS 150, FIRMS 90; `FRESHNESS_STALE_AFTER_MINUTES_<SOURCE>`). | It needs no new table. Staleness follows the job, not the data age: a healthy FIRMS feed can go hours without a detection, and a partial NWS run still wrote data. |
